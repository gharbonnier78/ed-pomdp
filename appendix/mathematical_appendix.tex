\section{Belief update with latent evidence quality}
Let $x=(s,e,\theta)$. For an evidence action $a$ and observation $o$,
\[
b'(s',e',\theta)\propto Z(o\mid s',e',a,\theta)\sum_{s,e}T(s',e'\mid s,e,a,\theta)b(s,e,\theta).
\]
When evidence quality is static during acquisition, $e'=e$; when remediation changes the environment or oracle, $T$ models that transition.
\section{Decision loss and abstention}
Terminal decisions minimise posterior expected loss over the feasible set. Abstention has explicit cost but prevents forced confidence. Under model uncertainty, robust risk is
\[
\rho_{rob}(d,b)=\sup_{\theta\in\Theta_b}\mathbb{E}[L(d,S)\mid b,\theta].
\]
\section{Matched-budget evaluation}
For budget $B$, policy performance is
\[
J_B(\pi)=\mathbb{E}[L(d_\tau,S)]\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \mathbb{E}\sum_{t<\tau}c(a_t)\le B.
\]
Policies are compared across a preregistered grid of budgets. Pareto dominance requires no greater cost and no greater loss, with at least one strict improvement.
\section{Calibration}
Calibration is assessed using Brier score, expected calibration error, reliability diagrams, and decision calibration: predicted Bayes risk versus realised loss. Classical calibration can be good while decision calibration is poor under asymmetric losses.
\section{Dependence generator}
Synthetic evidence dependence is generated through latent common causes $U$: $P(o_1,o_2\mid s)=\sum_uP(u\mid s)P(o_1\mid s,u)P(o_2\mid s,u)$. The naive model omits $U$; the dependence-aware model includes or marginalises it.
\section{Computational questions}
Exact POMDP planning is generally intractable at realistic scale. The programme compares myopic DVoI, rollout, point-based methods, and Monte Carlo planning \cite{silver2010pomcp}. Engineering feasibility may favour bounded-horizon or hierarchical policies over theoretical optimality.
\section{Audit replay invariant}
Given immutable evidence payload hashes, model version, policy version, configuration, and deterministic seed, replay must reproduce the recommended action and reason code exactly. Probabilistic solvers must persist sampled trajectories or use deterministic pseudo-random streams.
