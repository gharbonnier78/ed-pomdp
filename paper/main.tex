\begin{abstract}
Software release decisions are made under persistent partial observability: latent defects, uncertain residual risks, incomplete tests, non-representative environments, and imperfect runtime evidence. Existing release governance often aggregates pass rates and coverage indicators without explicitly modelling how evidence quality, dependence, cost, admissibility, and freshness affect the decision. This paper introduces an Evidence-Driven Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (ED-POMDP) as a research framework for sequentially acquiring decision-relevant evidence and selecting release actions under hard constraints. The paper deliberately presents a preregistered methods and research programme rather than a validated operational model. It defines falsifiable hypotheses, matched-budget evaluation, misspecification and dependence experiments, constraint semantics, and an industrial validation path centred on software release assurance. Biometrics and unmanned aerial perception are retained only as future external-validity domains. The principal open question is whether observation and evidence-quality models can be identified and calibrated from realistic assurance data with sufficient robustness for decision support.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and motivation}
Release status is not directly observable. A release may pass every executed test and still contain a critical defect; conversely, failed tests may reflect an invalid environment or oracle rather than product risk. Requirements, architecture reviews, tests, traces, incidents, and expert judgements are observations generated through evidence-producing processes of unequal quality. Release governance therefore combines two forms of uncertainty: uncertainty about the system and uncertainty about the evidence system.

The proposed research asks whether release assurance can be formulated as a constrained sequential decision problem in which each evidence-acquisition action is evaluated by its expected reduction in decision loss rather than by coverage or entropy alone. The work does not claim that POMDPs are new, nor that a synthetic demonstrator establishes operational effectiveness. Its intended novelty lies in making governed engineering evidence a first-class decision-theoretic object.

\subsection{Contributions}
This Step-1 package contributes: (1) a software-centred ED-POMDP formalisation; (2) an explicit separation between latent system state and latent evidence-quality state; (3) preregistered matched-budget hypotheses and refutation criteria; (4) hard constraint semantics; (5) a research programme from synthetic falsification to retrospective replay and shadow deployment; and (6) an auditable evidence ontology linked to STRAT-Q.

\section{Research questions}
\begin{description}
\item[RQ1] Can software release assurance be represented as a constrained sequential decision problem under partial observability without collapsing quality into a single score?
\item[RQ2] Can latent evidence quality be estimated separately from latent system risk?
\item[RQ3] Does decision-aware value of information outperform fixed and entropy-based acquisition under matched budgets?
\item[RQ4] How sensitive are decisions to prior error, likelihood misspecification, dependence, and distribution shift?
\item[RQ5] Which hard constraints must remain non-compensatory, and how should violations be represented?
\item[RQ6] Can human review be calibrated as a context-dependent evidence source without treating people as infallible sensors?
\item[RQ7] Which parts of the model are identifiable from industrial data, and which require elicitation or conservative bounds?
\item[RQ8] Can recommendations be made auditable enough for engineering and executive governance?
\end{description}

\section{Related work and gap}
POMDPs provide a standard framework for planning under uncertain state and observations \cite{kaelbling1998planning,lauri2023robotics}. Active classification and active feature acquisition explicitly trade information against acquisition costs \cite{wu2020active,rahbar2025active}. Penetration testing has been modelled as information-gathering planning under incomplete network knowledge \cite{sarraute2013hackers}. Software reliability models estimate residual failure behaviour and release timing \cite{wong2024revisiting,ieee1633}, while Bayesian networks support causal and evidential reasoning \cite{fenton2012risk}. These lines remain fragmented with respect to provenance, admissibility, environment representativity, correlation, auditability, and release gates.

The common formalism must buy more than software reuse. It provides a disciplined separation of state uncertainty, evidence-process uncertainty, decision loss, and non-compensatory constraints; exposes when a test cannot influence the decision; supports counterfactual analysis; and produces a replayable decision trace. These are the scientific utilities to be tested, not assumed.

\section{Conceptual model}
At time $t$, the partially observable state is factorised as
\[
X_t=(S_t,E_t,C_t,\Theta_t),
\]
where $S_t$ is latent system risk, $E_t$ is latent evidence-production quality, $C_t$ is the observable decision context, and $\Theta_t$ contains uncertain model parameters. The belief is
\[
b_t(x)=P(S_t,E_t,\Theta_t\mid h_t,C_t),
\]
with history $h_t=(a_0,o_1,\ldots,a_{t-1},o_t)$.

An evidence item is not identical to an observation. An evidence action $a$ invokes a process with provenance, environment, oracle, data, and instrumentation. It produces an observation $o$ through $Z(o\mid S,E,a,\Theta)$. Consequently, a passing test in a stale or non-representative environment may provide little support for a production claim.

\section{Formal ED-POMDP}
We define
\[
\mathcal{M}=\langle\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O},T,Z,L,\gamma,\mathcal{K},\Pi\rangle.
\]
$\mathcal{A}$ includes evidence acquisition, remediation, escalation, staged deployment, GO, CONDITIONAL GO, NO-GO, and abstention. $L$ is a vector of decision, delay, acquisition, and operational losses. $\mathcal{K}$ is a set of hard constraints; $\Pi$ is an evidence provenance and audit model.

The Bayesian update is
\[
b_{t+1}(x')=\eta\,Z(o_{t+1}\mid x',a_t)\sum_x T(x'\mid x,a_t)b_t(x).
\]
For a terminal decision $d$, Bayes risk is
\[
\rho(d,b)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim b}[L(d,x)].
\]
A one-step decision-relevant value of information is
\[
\operatorname{DVoI}(a;b)=\rho^*(b)-c(a)-\mathbb{E}_{o\sim P(o\mid b,a)}[\rho^*(\tau(b,a,o))],
\]
where $\rho^*(b)=\min_{d\in D_{\mathcal K}(b)}\rho(d,b)$ and $D_{\mathcal K}(b)$ excludes inadmissible decisions.

\subsection{Hard constraints}
Hard constraints are not large scalar penalties. Examples include mandatory security evidence, regulatory artefacts, segregation of duties, or prohibition of GO above a critical-risk probability bound. A decision is feasible only if
\[
g_j(b,h,\Pi)\leq 0,\quad j=1,\dots,m.
\]
The demonstrator in Step 2 must instantiate at least: mandatory evidence before GO, an abstention rule under model uncertainty, and a conditional-go compensating-control requirement.

\section{Evidence ontology}
Each evidence item is represented by
\[
e=(id,type,claim,source,t,env,data,oracle,coverage,q,dep,adm,cost,hash).
\]
The quality vector $q$ includes validity, reliability, representativity, freshness, completeness, and reproducibility. Dependence $dep$ links shared generators, datasets, components, environments, and human reviewers. Admissibility $adm$ captures policy and regulatory use. Hashes and lineage support replay.

\section{Experimental protocol}
Step 2 will use a factorial benchmark varying budget, prior error, likelihood sample size, observation correlation, distribution shift, evidence freshness, and constraint regimes. Policies are compared at matched budgets and matched target risk. Primary outcomes are terminal decision loss and constraint violations; secondary outcomes are evidence cost, time to decision, calibration, abstention, posterior entropy, and computational cost. Confidence intervals, multiple seeds, preregistered analysis scripts, and ablations are mandatory.

The generative likelihood $Z_{true}$ and inference likelihood $\widehat Z$ must differ in misspecification experiments. No headline result may be based solely on an oracle model. Correlation is generated through common hidden causes. Sensitivity analyses vary loss ratios and the cost conversion coefficient instead of fixing a favourable scalarisation.

\section{Industrial validation path}
The software-release domain is primary. Phase 1 curates historical release evidence with anonymised identifiers. Phase 2 estimates conservative observation models on training releases. Phase 3 performs blinded retrospective replay on held-out releases. Phase 4 runs shadow recommendations without authority to change release decisions. Phase 5 evaluates prospective decision support under human governance. Biometrics and UAV are external-validity studies only after the software claims survive these phases.

\section{Threats to validity}
Construct validity is threatened by treating ``risk'' as a small categorical variable. Internal validity is threatened by hand-set likelihoods and shared assumptions between simulator and inference. Statistical validity is threatened by policy tuning on evaluation scenarios. External validity is limited until real releases are replayed. Operational validity is threatened by missing evidence, policy changes, and strategic behaviour. Ethical and governance risks include automation bias, opaque loss elicitation, and misuse of confidence estimates as guarantees. The programme addresses these through conservative intervals, blinded splits, immutable provenance, human sign-off, abstention, and explicit non-claims.

\section{Non-claims}
This paper does not establish that likelihoods are identifiable in practice, that ED-POMDP is operationally superior, that one common kernel is optimal across domains, or that a posterior probability constitutes a certification. It proposes a falsifiable framework and a validation programme.

\section{Conclusion}
ED-POMDP reframes release assurance as governed sequential evidence acquisition under partial observability. The Step-1 contribution is a precise, reviewable scientific foundation. The next milestone is not wider rhetoric but controlled experiments designed to disconfirm the proposed advantages.
